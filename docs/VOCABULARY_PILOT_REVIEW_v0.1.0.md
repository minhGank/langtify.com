## Specific corrections and evidence

The following recommendations distinguish valid French from misleading scope. A natural translation need not cover every sense of its English counterpart. These are proposed editorial changes, not changes to the manifest.

- **Whole loaf:** pain is natural French but does not itself exclude slices. Prefer the descriptive label **loaf of bread / le pain entier** for this existing whole-loaf sense, with a whole-loaf photo. Do not use pain de mie, which would restrict the bread type. This is an editorial scope proposal requiring human confirmation, not a claim that pain is incorrect.
- **Body towel:** propose **bath towel / la serviette de bain** with matching size/use guidance, rather than a broad English towel paired with body-specific French. A hand towel would then be outside the proposed narrower sense; confirm that boundary before changing identity or content.
- **Pliers:** propose **combination pliers / la pince universelle**, already the tool requested by the photo prompt. Narrow the definition as well as the label. English plural and French singular are normal here, not a grammar error.
- **Canadian English:** prefer **baseboard**, **downspout** and **drawer slide** over skirting board, drainpipe and drawer runner for the specified senses. OQLF labels skirting board British and gives baseboard alongside plinthe; Canada's flood guidance uses downspout; Lee Valley's Canadian catalog uses drawer slides. Keep stable concept keys. Sources: [OQLF plinthe](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8360884/plinthe), [Canada.ca downspouts](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/policing/emergencies/preparedness/get-prepared/hazards-emergencies/floods/flood-risk/prevent-flood-damage/eavestroughs-and-downspouts.html), [Lee Valley slides](https://www.leevalley.com/en-ca/shop/hardware/slides).
- **Everyday versus formal French:** fermeture à glissière is correct, not an unnatural mistranslation. Keep the existing familiar alternative fermeture éclair visible in editorial metadata. It is not specific to Canada; the Académie documents the established name and its trademark origin. Do not raise the concept's CEFR simply because its primary label is formal. [Académie française](https://www.dictionnaire-academie.fr/article/A9F0501).
- **Specialist senses:** constrain loquet to the simple pivoting latch, distinct from the fixed gâche; keep the paintbrush sleeve sense of virole. Passepoil can denote edging beyond raised corded upholstery, so the latter needs a qualifier and specialist review. Sources: [Larousse loquet](https://www.larousse.fr/dictionnaires/francais/loquet/47799), [Usito virole](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/virole), [OQLF passepoil](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2073255/passepoil), [French government textile terminology](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/files/files/directions_services/daj/media-document/articles-confectionnes-terminologie.pdf). These references establish senses, not C1/C2 classifications.

No definite article/gender error was found in the 60 primary French forms. Most problems concern specificity, regional preference or photo boundaries. The automated grammar baseline is not independent linguistic certification. The proposed unhyphenated bouton bûchette and removal of the broad grommet alias still need editorial acceptance; neither is silently changed.

## Evidence and limitations

CEFR describes proficiency, not automatic levels for object names: [Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors). Dictionary and manufacturer references support meanings or regional usage only. All per-term CEFR values remain unverified estimates; original definitions are not copied dictionary text. No independent human approval or physical-device photo testing is claimed.
